Helpology

An automated medication dispenser that keeps dosing on schedule and keeps caregivers connected, remotely.

Developed from a device concept patented by sponsor Mindy Minhchau Le (US 2025/0082546 A1), now in its third generation of Senior Design development.

Background

As the population ages, managing daily medication has become an increasingly common challenge for older adults and the people who care for them. Many older adults live with multiple chronic conditions and take several medications each day — a pattern known as polypharmacy — and when combined with cognitive decline or impaired vision, this frequently leads to missed doses, accidental double dosing, and incorrect medication. The burden of preventing these outcomes often falls on family or professional caregivers, who cannot always be present for every dose.

Helpology is based on a device concept patented by project sponsor Mindy Minhchau Le (US Patent Publication 2025/0082546 A1), developed from her own experience managing medication for her mother. The sponsor’s long-term goal is to bring the device to a state suitable for FDA submission as a medical device. Helpology is now in its third generation of development across successive Senior Design teams, each building on the work of the last; our team’s focus has been resolving reliability issues in the electrical and software subsystems while preserving the working mechanical and cooling systems built by prior teams.

Key Requirements

These requirements were defined directly by the sponsor from her own caregiving experience, alongside formal timing and safety specifications in the project’s System Requirements Specification.

  • Dose verification (carousel): a sensor or camera confirms whether a dispensed dose remains in the cup or was removed.
  • Dose verification (eye-drop compartments): compartments open only when due, close after a timeout, and confirm bottle return.
  • Motion-triggered event recording: short clips are captured on motion within 3–6 feet, rather than continuously; facial recognition is not used.
  • Caregiver–patient communication: the caregiver can speak to the patient in real time through the device’s screen and speaker.
  • Patient reminders: toggleable instructions escalate from a spoken prompt to a flashing light to an alarm.
  • Caregiver-initiated dose hold: feasibility of holding the next dose when the current one is unretrieved, without creating a missed-dose risk.
  • Video retention and privacy: event video is retained roughly 7 days by default, with caregiver view, save, and delete access; deleting a video never deletes its medication event record.

Core timing specifications: dosing events begin within 5 seconds of their schedule, even without network connectivity; compartment state is confirmed within 1 second; and the refrigerated compartment is held between 2–8°C.

Architectural Design

Helpology’s architecture is organized into three independent layers, connected only through defined interfaces, so that a network outage never prevents a scheduled dose from being delivered.

Implementation Details

Hardware
Raspberry Pi 4 (Raspberry Pi OS) driving a DC-motor-and-encoder carousel, a 3″ linear actuator with limit switch for the compartment door, a USB camera with embedded speaker/mic, radiator-and-fan cooling, and a 7×3″ HDMI/micro-USB touchscreen.
Software
Scheduling, actuator control, and event logging run in Python and C on-device, persisted to a local SQLite database so medication is never missed during a network outage; video is buffered to USB storage if the cloud is unreachable.
Cloud & Mobile
Firebase (Cloud Functions, Firestore, Storage, Cloud Messaging) handles event records, video, and push notifications; a companion Android app lets caregivers schedule medication, review history, and send real-time voice messages, with notifications mirrored to a paired smartwatch.

Conclusions

Helpology’s design closely matches the sponsor’s goals: reliable scheduled dispensing with confirmed, not assumed, retrieval, plus remote caregiver visibility. Our team’s main contribution was improving reliability left incomplete by prior teams — camera-verified retrieval, a consolidated Firebase backend, and a working Android app. Scope was narrowed, with sponsor approval, to prioritize reliability within budget and timeline.
